# Lab 2: DMM Ohmmeter Measurement and the Breadboard — Pre-Lab Questions

## Q1. What is the purpose of a Digital Multimeter?
measure electrical properties of things such as voltage, current, and resistence, also used for ensuring circuits are working 
## Q2. When measuring resistance using a DMM, should the component or circuit be powered or unpowered? Briefly state your reason.
Unpowered, it uses own small test voltage or current to find the resistance, if unkown power like a battery is used reading will probably wrong or not as accurate

## Q3. Which resistance-measurement mode, 2W (two-wire) or 4W (four-wire), is generally more accurate when measuring very small resistances? Briefly explain.
4W measurement is more accurate, 2 wires force a test current and two separate wires get the voltage directly at the component. This avoids including the resistance of the test leads and contact points in the result

## Q4. In a 2W (two-wire) resistance measurement, if the two DMM test leads are clipped together, would you expect the measured resistance to be exactly 0 Ω? Briefly explain.
No, the reading will be a small nonzero value because the leads, clips, and their contact resistance have resistance meter accuracy may not be right either

## Q5. A resistor is labeled 1 kΩ with a tolerance of 5%. What is the minimum possible resistance?
$R_{\min}=1000\ \Omega(1-0.05)=950\ \Omega.$
The minimum possible resistance is **950 Ω** (0.95 kΩ)

## Q6. A resistor is labeled 1 kΩ with a tolerance of 5%. What is the maximum possible resistance?
$R_{\max}=1000\ \Omega(1+0.05)=1050\ \Omega.$
The maximum possible resistance is **1050 Ω** (1.05 kΩ)

## Q7. What does the continuity function of a DMM test?
It tests whether there is a low-resistance electrical path or connection between the two probes

## Q8. In continuity mode, what does it usually mean when the DMM beeps?
It usually means the probes are connected through a sufficiently low resistance, so the circuit path is continuous
