## 1

The $7.5\ \text{k}\Omega$ resistor is in series with the parallel combination.

$R_{BC}=3.3\parallel10=\dfrac{(3.3)(10)}{3.3+10}=2.481\ \text{k}\Omega$

$R_{\text{eq}}=7.5+2.481=9.981\ \text{k}\Omega$

$I_s=\dfrac{20\ \text{V}}{9.981\ \text{k}\Omega}=2.004\ \text{mA}$

The supply current is $2.004\ \text{mA}$ from the positive terminal toward node A.

## 2

$V_{AB}=I_s(7.5\ \text{k}\Omega)$

$V_{AB}=(2.003766\ \text{mA})(7.5\ \text{k}\Omega)=15.028\ \text{V}$

## 3

$V_{BC}=I_sR_{BC}$

$V_{BC}=(2.003766\ \text{mA})(2.481203\ \text{k}\Omega)=4.972\ \text{V}$

Check: $V_{AB}+V_{BC}=15.028+4.972=20.000\ \text{V}$

## 4

$I_{3.3\text{k}}=\dfrac{V_{BC}}{3.3\ \text{k}\Omega}$

$I_{3.3\text{k}}=\dfrac{4.971751\ \text{V}}{3.3\ \text{k}\Omega}=1.507\ \text{mA}$

The current flows from B to C.

## 5

$I_{10\text{k}}=\dfrac{V_{BC}}{10\ \text{k}\Omega}$

$I_{10\text{k}}=\dfrac{4.971751\ \text{V}}{10\ \text{k}\Omega}=0.497\ \text{mA}$

The current flows from B to C.

Check: $I_{3.3\text{k}}+I_{10\text{k}}=1.507+0.497=2.004\ \text{mA}$

## 6

The branches below node B end at the open node C, so they do not affect the resistance measured from A to B.

$R_{AB}=7.500\ \text{k}\Omega$

## 7

The $7.5\ \text{k}\Omega$ branch ends at the open node A, so it does not affect the resistance measured from B to C.

$R_{BC}=3.3\parallel10$

$R_{BC}=\dfrac{(3.3)(10)}{3.3+10}=2.481\ \text{k}\Omega$

## 8

From A to C, the $7.5\ \text{k}\Omega$ resistor is in series with $3.3\ \text{k}\Omega\parallel10\ \text{k}\Omega$.

$R_{AC}=7.5+(3.3\parallel10)$

$R_{AC}=7.5+2.481203=9.981\ \text{k}\Omega$

## 9

Shorting A to C connects all three resistors between B and C. They are in parallel.

$R_{BC}=7.5\parallel3.3\parallel10$

$\dfrac{1}{R_{BC}}=\dfrac{1}{7.5}+\dfrac{1}{3.3}+\dfrac{1}{10}$

$R_{BC}=1.864\ \text{k}\Omega$

## 10

In Figure 2, node A is open. Therefore, no test current can flow through the $7.5\ \text{k}\Omega$ branch when resistance is measured between B and C. The meter measures only $3.3\ \text{k}\Omega\parallel10\ \text{k}\Omega=2.481\ \text{k}\Omega$.

In Figure 3, shorting A to C completes the $7.5\ \text{k}\Omega$ branch between B and C. The meter then measures $7.5\ \text{k}\Omega\parallel3.3\ \text{k}\Omega\parallel10\ \text{k}\Omega=1.864\ \text{k}\Omega$. Adding a parallel path decreases the equivalent resistance.
