Valid and Invalid Arguments
### Argument forms

| Modus Ponens (prove valid) | Modus Tollens (prove invalid) | Contradiction rule                                                       |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| $p \to q$                  | $p \to q$                     | $\sim p \to  c$ or $\neg p \to  \bot$                                    |
| $p$                        | $\sim q$                      | $\therefore p$                                                           |
| $\therefore q$             | $\therefore \sim p$           | if you assuming something leads to a contradiction, its opposite is true |

| Elimination    | Specialization | Conjunction             | Generlization          | Transitivity          | Division into cases      |
| -------------- | -------------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------- | --------------------- | ------------------------ |
| $p \lor  q$    | $p \land q$    | $p$                     | $p$                    | $p \to q$             | $p \lor  q$              |
| $\sim  q$      | $\therefore p$ | $q$                     |                        | $q \to  r$            | $p \to  r$ and $q \to r$ |
| $\therefore p$ | $\therefore q$ | $\therefore p \land  q$ | $\therefore p \lor  q$ | $\therefore p \to  r$ | $\therefore r$           |

> [!example]- Example Question and Answer
> given these statements build up an argument:
> Statements:
> 1. $p \lor  q$
> 2. $q \to r$
> 3. $p \land  s \to t$
> 4. $\sim r$
> 5. $\sim q \to u \land  s$
> 6. $\therefore t$
> 
> Argument:
> $q \to r$ by `2`
> $\sim  r$ by `4`
> `(1)` $\therefore \sim q$ `by Modus Tollens`
> 
> $p \lor q$ `true by 1`
> $\sim q$ `true by (1)`
> `(2)` $\therefore p$ `by elimination`
> 
> $\sim q \to u \land  s$ `by 5`
> $\sim q$ `by (1)`
> `(3)` $\therefore u \land  s$ `by Modus Ponens`
> $\therefore s$ `by specialization`
> 
> $p$ `by (2)`
> `(5)` $\therefore p \land  s$ `by conjunction`
> $p \land s \to t$ `by 3`
> $\therefore t$ `by modus ponens`

>[!example]- knights and knaves problem
>Come accross 2 people $A$ and $B$
>A says: $B$ is a knight
>B says: $A$ and I are of opposite types
>
>we contruct an argument via contradiction, take an argument as true and attempt contradiction
>
>suppose: B is a knight
>$\therefore$ what B says is true, `by definition of knight`
>$\therefore$ A and B are of opposite types `by what B said`
>$\therefore$ A is not a knight `by what B said`
>$\therefore$ A is a knave `by elimination`
>$\therefore$ What A says is false `by definition of a knave`
>$\therefore$ B is not a knight `by the negation of what a said`
>$\therefore$ B is a knave `by elimination`
>$\therefore$ B is a knight and not a knight 
>$\therefore$ the supposition is false `by contradiction rule`
>$\therefore$ B is not a knight `by negation of the supposition`
>$\therefore$ B is a knave `by elimination`
>$\therefore$ what B says is false `by definition of a knave`
>$\therefore$ A and B are of the same type `by negation of what b said`
>$\therefore$ A and B are of both knaves `by negation of what b said`
>$\therefore$ A is a knave `by specialization`
