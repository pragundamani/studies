###### 2. Predicate $P$
[[3_3_textbook_exercises#Question 3.3.2|Question]]

#### Let $P(x,y)$ mean $x<y$ on the real numbers. $\forall x \exists y\, P(x,y)$ is **true**. $\exists y \forall x\, P(x,y)$ is **false**.

###### 3. Nonzero-number predicate

###### 5. Tarski world: circle and same-colour square

###### 6. Tarski world: circle, square, and above

###### 11. Students and movies

###### 12. Statements over $D$
[[3_3_textbook_exercises#Question 3.3.12|Question]]

#### Let $D=\{1,2\}$. Negation of $\forall x \in D\, (x>0)$ is $\exists x \in D\, (x \le 0)$, which is **false**, so the original is **true**. Negation of $\exists x \in D\, (x>1)$ is $\forall x \in D\, (x \le 1)$, which is **false** because of 2, so the original is **true**.

###### 13. Colours and animals

#### **(a)** Every colour is the colour of at least one animal. **(b)** There is a colour that no animal has.

###### 14. A book read by every person
[[3_3_textbook_exercises#Question 3.3.14|Question]]

#### **(a)** Some book has been read by everyone. **(b)** No book has been read by everyone; equivalently, every book has at least one person who has not read it.

###### 15. Odd integers
[[3_3_textbook_exercises#Question 3.3.15|Question]]

#### There is an odd integer $x$ for which there is an integer $y$ with $x=2y+1$. The negation: every odd integer $x$ fails $x=2y+1$ for every integer $y$, which is false.

###### 16. Real numbers
[[3_3_textbook_exercises#Question 3.3.16|Question]]

#### $\forall x \exists y\, (y>x)$ says every real is less than some real. Negation: $\exists x \forall y\, (y \le x)$, some real is a maximum. The original is **true**.

###### 17. Integers
[[3_3_textbook_exercises#Question 3.3.17|Question]]

#### $\forall m \exists n\, (n=m+1)$: every integer has a successor. Negation: $\exists m \forall n\, (n \neq m+1)$. The original is **true**.

###### 18. Real numbers
[[3_3_textbook_exercises#Question 3.3.18|Question]]

#### There is a real $y$ such that $y^2=2$. Negation: for every real $y$, $y^2 \neq 2$, which is false. The original is **true**.

###### 19. Real numbers
[[3_3_textbook_exercises#Question 3.3.19|Question]]

#### For every real $x$, $x^2 \ge 0$. Negation: there is a real $x$ with $x^2<0$. The claim is **true**.

###### 24. Negation rules
[[3_3_textbook_exercises#Question 3.3.24|Question]]

#### Negation of $\forall x \exists y\, P(x,y)$ is $\exists x \forall y\, \sim P(x,y)$. Negation of $\exists x \forall y\, P(x,y)$ is $\forall x \exists y\, \sim P(x,y)$.

###### 37. Even integers
[[3_3_textbook_exercises#Question 3.3.37|Question]]

#### Answer 3.3.37

$$\forall n\in\mathbb Z\,(\operatorname{Even}(n)\to\exists k\in\mathbb Z\,(n=2k)).$$

Its negation is $\exists n\in\mathbb Z\,(\operatorname{Even}(n)\land\forall k\in\mathbb Z\,(n\ne2k))$.

###### 45. Eliminating $\exists !$
[[3_3_textbook_exercises#Question 3.3.45|Question]]

#### (a) **True.** $x=1$ works, and if $xy=y$ for every $y$, then $2x=2$, so $x=1$.

#### (b) **False.** Both $x=1$ and $x=-1$ work.

#### (c) **True.** For each $x$, $y=-x$ is the only solution.

#### For the intended statement “there exists a unique $x\in D$ such that $P(x)$,” write

$$\exists x\in D\,\bigl(P(x)\land\forall y\in D\,(P(y)\to y=x)\bigr).$$

###### 55. Same-truth-value forms
[[3_3_textbook_exercises#Question 3.3.55|Question]]

#### **Not always the same.** On $D=\{1,2\}$, let $P$ and $Q$ hold only at 1. Then $\exists x\,(P(x) \land Q(x))$ is true, but $\forall x\, P(x)$ is false, so $\forall x\, P(x) \land \exists x\, Q(x)$ is false.

###### 56. Same-truth-value forms
[[3_3_textbook_exercises#Question 3.3.56|Question]]

#### **Not always the same.** Take $D=\{1,2,3\}$, $P(x)$ even, and $Q(x)$ odd. No element is both, so the first form is false, but $2$ is even and $1$ is odd, so the second is true.

###### 57. Same-truth-value forms
[[3_3_textbook_exercises#Question 3.3.57|Question]]

#### **Not always the same.** (3.3.57)

Take $D=\{1,2,3\}$, $P(x)$ even, and $Q(x)$ odd. Every element is even or odd, so $\forall x\,(P(x)\lor Q(x))$ is true, but neither predicate holds for every element.

###### 58. Same-truth-value forms
[[3_3_textbook_exercises#Question 3.3.58|Question]]

#### **Not always the same.** (3.3.58) $\exists x \forall y\, P(x,y)$ implies $\forall y \exists x\, P(x,y)$, but not conversely. Let $P(x,y)$ mean $x<y$ on the reals: the second is true and the first is false.
